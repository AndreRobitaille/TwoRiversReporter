# Developing election-time features

Voters come with three questions. Who’s running? What are they running on? Should I vote for them? Our strongest reporting compares what the three Council members whose terms are ending promised in their last campaigns with what they did in office.

**Keep the year-round page useful.** A resident who comes for current members or Council business should find both on the first screen or one obvious tap away. On phones, place one collapsed election guide immediately above the roster. On wide screens, show it open beside the roster. Links from the homepage and member profiles open the guide directly. Each incumbent’s name links to their individual profile.

Every member on the current City Council roster uses the same profile layout year-round. Show the member’s office, available campaign reporting, and Council work with vote search, attendance, committees and a link to the full record. A saved term assessment adds follow-through findings. When an assessment is pending, show available campaign statements from that term and link to the evidence.

**On the three seat holders’ profiles**, give the role, spring term end, election dates and official contact link. Show the term-wide result count and pace alongside the full campaign and in-office overview. Make the missed promises visible on arrival or by scrolling. Beside the relevant promise, show the action, Council’s vote and the date as compact text. Give consequential actions, what Council decided, and any material limits on what changed. Put the sources for the story in one expandable list. Residents can find a vote, check attendance or see current committees from the same profile. A link from a specific vote carries that decision with it.

The Tuesday 11 p.m. Central discovery run checks sitting Council members with a verified service term and tracked campaign positions after incoming records have been processed. The election guide features the three seats whose terms are ending. Search Council and committee minutes across the member’s term alongside accepted action and vote records. Compare the material evidence before reserving a generation request. Each member can use at most one request in that cycle, within the standing Council allowance. Page views use the saved result. The job reuses an unchanged assessment, defers when the standing allowance is unavailable, and validates source references before publishing a changed assessment. A work-session position reported only in a recording can still need editorial review. Until a featured member has a sourced overview, show a short excerpt of the last campaign and links to the underlying records.

## What voters need as the election approaches

- **During filing**, a voter may have heard only one name. Identify the three members whose terms end in the election year. Keep a fixed timeline for that election showing when signature gathering begins, the regular filing deadline and Election Day. Use the Clerk’s dates. [City Clerk’s filing instructions](https://www.two-rivers.org/clerk/page/ballot-access-procedure-and-documents-required)
- **When the candidate list is available**, choose **Add candidates** under **Needs you** in Admin → Elections. Use the first list available to the editor or the first HTR or Seehafer article that lists the field. An editor can correct or remove names from the saved list. The guide shows the names and links to the Clerk for the official ballot. Date individual campaign statements where they appear on member records.
- **Near Election Day**, the term overview should help voters weigh consequential promises and what the member did about them. For the regular three-seat race, say “You may vote for up to three people. You can choose just one or two.”

The currently configured guide switches to the next election the Tuesday one week after Election Day. The proposed annual schedule below extends results coverage and opens the following season in November. Use the year in the guide and candidate headings so someone arriving in April can tell which spring the names are for. Identify members whose terms end and give readers the [City Clerk’s election information](https://www.two-rivers.org/clerk/page/election-information).

## Did the member follow through?

For a past election, review answers from the members who won seats. The 2024
profiles for Bittner, Brandt and Derby assess how each answered the same two
questions about comparable cities and preparation on local business issues.
Lead the member profile with the term score and in-office findings. On desktop,
keep the promise breakdown in the main column and put the forum findings
directly below **Council work** in the sidebar. On narrow screens, the reading
order is follow-through, Council work, Campaign promises, then the forum findings.
Council work opens expanded, with vote search, committees and attendance visible.
Readers can collapse it. Use a distinct background at each section change so
someone scrolling can find their place.
Use **Did [surname] Answer?** only when a reviewed exchange shows a specific
shortcoming a voter should know about. Name what the question asked for and
what the answer left unresolved. Link separately to the question and the
member’s response. Leave routine, responsive answers in the source material.
Omit the section and its navigation when there is no finding to show. On the
profile, show a proposal once when its scored finding already covers it.
Only the reviewed platform supplies the promise score.

Use the existing consolidated platform as the complete list for the weekly assessment. The 2024 review retained 12 items for Bittner, 10 for Brandt and 10 for Derby. Brandt presented two rental rules as alternatives in one answer, so the 32 items represent 31 distinct commitments or positions. Excluded campaign remarks stay out of all resident lists, direct statement links and summary inputs. Preserve the member's words and conditions. Give each retained position the meaning the member gave it, whether it was a proposal, priority or pledge. Combine repeat mentions across sources. A preference or general aspiration does not enter the count. Credit requires the member's own action and a documented result proportionate to the undertaking. Distinguish an adopted rule, a funded contract and a completed project. For a promised outcome, a pending action or a losing vote earns zero result credit. For a promise to introduce a measure, making the motion can itself deliver the undertaking. Identify the vote and what happened afterward.

Give one point for a documented result. Deduct 2.5 points for a personally attributable action materially against the position. Deduct half a point for a missed opportunity, a documented shortfall or a promise unfulfilled in the public record. A recorded effort with an open outcome or a losing vote carries zero points. Apply one verdict per position and round only the final percentage. Divide the net points by **every distinct tracked promise**. Label the percentage “promise score.” Show the arithmetic and each deduction beside it. A red −2.5 links to the action, the result and its sources. A negative score remains visible below zero. Use a distinct label for a documented attempt, an outvoted effort, a missed opportunity, a contrary action, or a gap in evidence. A missed opportunity requires a cited review identifying a concrete occasion to act and no recorded public follow-through on that promise. Look for later action before assigning that finding. When the reviewed record does not document delivery, mark the promise “Unfulfilled in the public record.” It costs half a point and stays in the denominator. A claim that the member passed up a specific opportunity requires evidence of that opportunity. Work-session recordings can establish a named position that the minutes summarize collectively. Compare the direction of the actual decision with the specific campaign proposal. Distinguish a vote on the proposal from a decision that points away from its goal. An attributed action in a reviewed shortfall cannot supply credit for that shortfall. Check the wording of the motion and how the individual voted, including votes on repeals and denials. Review personal advocacy at work sessions and subsequent decisions. When evidence points in both directions, weigh and cite both. A newly identified vote can change a prior judgment even when the event predates the review.

For a verified regular term with a known scheduled turnover, also show the share of calendar days elapsed and days remaining. Two Rivers elects council members to three-year terms. The next council organizes on the third Tuesday of April. Compare the unrounded net score with an even pace over that interval. Green meets or exceeds the time elapsed, amber trails by up to ten percentage points and red trails by more. An incomplete record of delivery at term end falls short. These bands are editorial benchmarks. Individual promises can have different deadlines. With unknown dates, show the promise score without the term clock. With zero promises, omit both score and pace. The clock advances locally and has no effect on weekly AI eligibility. [City Council](https://www.two-rivers.org/citycouncil) · [Council rules on the organizational meeting](https://www.two-rivers.org/sites/default/files/fileattachments/city_council/meeting/packets/3279/city-council-packets-2020.04.27.pdf)

Keep the promise breakdown on the member page. Color the entire group heading and print the count, verdict and points. Use green for documented results, amber for documented attempts with an unconfirmed outcome or a losing vote, and red for missed, short, reversed and undocumented results. Both saved findings and subsequent Tuesday assessments use these same display rules. A reader who only scrolls can see what happened across the term. Open a finding for the full explanation, supporting records and exact campaign wording. Use a verbatim excerpt where it stands on its own and the complete finding elsewhere. Weigh activity over the whole term against the member's actual words. A binding decision, funded program or documented change can fulfill a specific commitment in one action. For a sustained priority, explain what the member's actions achieved over time.

- Repeated proposals, amendments or votes that materially advanced the pledge can uphold a commitment to pursue it. Report what Council ultimately decided and what changed for residents, including when the member lost.
- If the member cast only one or two minor votes in three years despite repeated opportunities, say they fell short and explain what those votes accomplished.
- If the relevant full-term record shows opportunities and no proposal, advocacy or vote for the promised action, say they did not keep the promise. Cite those opportunities. During an unfinished term, say what happened so far. Identify any missing evidence that could change the judgment.

Check the text and attachments the member actually voted to adopt. A work-session goal can document personal support. For funding, follow the applicant, financing instrument and scope through the award and any withdrawal. For a project, check aliases, the site, the original deal and later financing. Match accessibility claims to the barrier a funded or installed feature addresses.

A dated response to a resident or an amendment that changed a decision tells voters something about listening and independent judgment. A majority-vote percentage alone cannot explain someone’s contribution.

For substantive changes, give Frank and Dana the same entry page and a reason to visit appropriate to the season. Keep expected answers and ballot rules with the coordinator. Ask what the reader learned, what wording shaped their judgment, and where they stopped. Include an ordinary year-round task. An informed voter can still be undecided. ## Annual collection schedule proposed November 27

The historical backfill desk is available in local development. The recurring
seasonal workflow below is product direction. The current public guide has a
configured list of elections through spring 2029 and switches editions one
week after Election Day.

The HTR questionnaire check now has a Solid Queue schedule for March 1 through
Election Day. Running **Find HTR questionnaire** in an earlier edition uses
the same job. A publisher restriction or an unfamiliar article layout leaves
the article on the editor’s desk for a permitted copy.

The forum watcher checks imported Council agendas and minutes each morning from February 1 through Election Day. It matches named League of Women Voters and Two Rivers Business Association events to broadcasts on the City’s YouTube channel. **Find voter forums** runs the same search for the selected election. The editor steps in when a recording needs captions or the transcript is ready for speaker labels.

## Forums, questionnaires and family in local government

Put the finding in the candidate’s profile. Date the campaign, explain what
opportunities voters had to hear from that candidate, and link the records in
**Campaign sources**. When a returning candidate drops out of both forums and
questionnaires, explain what the prior answers leave open in the later race.
During the race, every published candidate name opens a profile, including a challenger who has
never served. The list supplies the name and year. Saved questionnaires,
verified forum passages, photos and public service records fill the same profile
as they arrive. When the app matches a challenger to a person, the original
candidate link opens that person’s profile. Editors can correct the shared
source or list without creating or republishing an individual page.
The municipal canvass closes each losing campaign. Unpublish the individual
candidate page and its search listing. Keep the field, results and source
material for the election desk. A winner keeps their profile. Someone who
served in another term or on a committee keeps that public service record.
A later filing opens the new campaign under the same person.

Count each forum once across recordings. A verified speaking passage establishes
participation. A named absence needs confirmation from the recording. In
**Admin → Elections**, open the saved forum and use **Confirm forum absences**
to record or clear it. An unchecked name without a speaking passage stays
unconfirmed. Reopening the profile uses the current saved material. For a
timed forum statement, link to the question and the candidate’s answer in the
recording when their passages can be located. Keep the caption excerpt available.
If someone answered a questionnaire and their positions have yet to be processed,
give the reader a direct link to the published answers.

A nonresponse applies to the whole questionnaire only when the publisher says
so for that candidate. A skipped question or missing copy does not establish a
nonresponse. Participation records carry no score or assumed explanation.
State family ties directly. When two people served together, give the public
body, their roles and the period. Link relevant public records in the source
disclosure. An editor can supply a relationship once for the people involved.
A new candidate needs no family note.

## Candidate photos

The election guide places a framed portrait to the right of the candidate’s
name, up to 80 pixels tall on phones and 96 on desktop. The member profile
uses the same photo at up to 144 and 176 pixels respectively and links the
publication credit beneath the name to the article. A challenger without a matched person record also has a
photo credit below the guide row. Both layouts show the entire image at its original
aspect ratio. The ordinary Council roster stays text-only.

In **Admin → Elections**, save the HTR or Seehafer questionnaire with its
named photos. Cataloged questionnaires accept a formatted paste or saved
article HTML. For a new questionnaire, enter each candidate’s name on its own
line above their answers and attach the saved article HTML.

The 13 known portraits from 2024–2026 have article and image links in the
election catalog, so a fresh installation can fetch them. The site keeps one
public thumbnail per person. When another saved article
includes their photo, the article’s publication date decides which image and
credit appear on both pages. An undated article sorts at the start of its
election year. An older backfill or a newer article without a photo leaves
the current image in place. While a replacement download is being retried,
readers can still see the previous photo. Each questionnaire retains its
original named image links alongside its answers.
