# Developing election-time features

Voters come with three questions. Who’s running? What are they running on? Should I vote for them? Our strongest reporting compares what the three Council members whose terms are ending promised in their last campaigns with what they did in office.

**Keep the year-round page useful.** A resident who comes for current members or Council business should find both on the first screen or one obvious tap away. On phones, place one collapsed election guide immediately above the roster. On wide screens, show it open beside the roster. Links from the homepage and member profiles open the guide directly. Each incumbent’s name links to their individual profile.

**On the three seat holders’ profiles**, give the role, spring term end, election dates and official contact link. Lead with a short, sourced account of the last campaign and the term so far. Give consequential actions, what Council decided, and any material limits on what changed. The resident can open the full campaign reporting or find a vote or meeting. A link from a specific vote carries that decision with it.

The Tuesday 11 p.m. Central discovery run checks sitting Council members with a verified service term and tracked campaign positions after incoming records have been processed. The election guide features the three seats whose terms are ending. Search Council and committee minutes across the member’s term alongside accepted action and vote records. Compare the material evidence before reserving a generation request. Each member can use at most one request in that cycle, within the standing Council allowance. Page views use the saved result. Until a featured member has a sourced overview, show a short excerpt of the last campaign and links to the underlying records.

## What voters need as the election approaches

- **During filing**, a voter may have heard only one name. Identify the three members whose terms end in the election year. Keep a fixed timeline for that election showing when signature gathering begins, the regular filing deadline and Election Day. Use the Clerk’s dates. [City Clerk’s filing instructions](https://www.two-rivers.org/clerk/page/ballot-access-procedure-and-documents-required)
- **As voters hear more names**, date each campaign statement. Give any confirmed candidacy its own source and direct readers to the Clerk for the official ballot.
- **Near Election Day**, the term overview should help voters weigh consequential promises and what the member did about them. For the regular three-seat race, say “You may vote for up to three people. You can choose just one or two.”

On the Tuesday one week after Election Day, switch to the next election’s dates and three seat holders. Use the year in the heading so someone arriving in April can tell which spring the guide covers. Put a short note beside the names.

> These members hold the seats up for election that year. We compare what they promised when they last ran with what they did in office. Meeting records may never name everyone running. For the official ballot once available, see the [City Clerk’s election information](https://www.two-rivers.org/clerk/page/election-information).

## Did the member follow through?

Use the existing consolidated platform as the complete list for the weekly assessment. The 2024 review retained 11 items for Bittner, 10 for Brandt and 10 for Derby. Brandt presented two rental rules as alternatives in one answer, so the 31 items represent 30 distinct commitments or positions. Excluded campaign remarks stay out of all resident lists, direct statement links and summary inputs. Preserve the member's words and conditions. Give each retained position the meaning the member gave it, whether it was a proposal, priority or pledge. Combine repeat mentions across sources. A preference or general aspiration does not enter the count. A promise earns a finding of follow-through or falling short only when directly relevant evidence establishes what the member did. Pending work and insufficient evidence stay unresolved.

Divide fulfilled promises by **every distinct campaign promise**. An unresolved promise counts in the denominator and earns zero credit. Below one-third, say how few promises the record shows fulfilled. From one-third through two-thirds, describe the record as mixed. Above two-thirds, lead with follow-through. For one or two tracked promises, give the count plainly. For zero promises, omit the percentage. Use the unrounded fraction for tone. In the breakdown, give the percentage, the member’s words, the latest action or absence of recorded action, and the sources for each finding.

Show each tracked promise, finding, short explanation and evidence links on the member page. The exact campaign wording can expand beneath it. Weigh activity over the whole term against the member's actual words. A binding decision, funded program or documented change can fulfill a specific commitment in one action. For a sustained priority, explain what the member's actions achieved over time.

- Repeated proposals, amendments or votes that materially advanced the pledge can uphold a commitment to pursue it. Report what Council ultimately decided and what changed for residents, including when the member lost.
- If the member cast only one or two minor votes in three years despite repeated opportunities, say they fell short and explain what those votes accomplished.
- If the relevant full-term record shows opportunities and no proposal, advocacy or vote for the promised action, say they did not keep the promise. Cite those opportunities. During an unfinished term, say what happened so far. Identify any missing evidence that could change the judgment.

A dated response to a resident or an amendment that changed a decision tells voters something about listening and independent judgment. A majority-vote percentage alone cannot explain someone’s contribution.

For substantive changes, give Frank and Dana the same entry page and a reason to visit appropriate to the season. Keep expected answers and ballot rules with the coordinator. Ask what the reader learned, what wording shaped their judgment, and where they stopped. Include an ordinary year-round task. An informed voter can still be undecided.
